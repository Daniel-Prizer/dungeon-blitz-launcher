# Experimental widescreen (0.18.0, minor)

Compatibility restored in 0.13.0 for the subsequently deployed live revision (`clientrev=swf-7a3486bf928a`, source SHA-256 `fdc215711de56246ce9188b71494c8b00664db66b530e9aabd51a760f9898255`). It has its own reviewed absolute offsets and independently compiled audio/display delta; both prior supported revisions remain supported. Main's layout and the focus-presentation method are whole-hash identical to the earlier client. Offline review also checks the semantic multiname and numeric constant-pool prefix, so equal instruction bytes are not accepted with different symbol bindings. The display builder separately validates terrain-cache and transition-allocator hashes and verifies unrelated method bodies remain unchanged. No upstream gameplay changes are reverted. Future unknown revisions stay unchanged and trigger a bounded signed-update check, rather than applying guessed patches.

Enable Settings > Display > Experimental 16:9. It starts off and saves your explicit choice. The outer decorative border adds 31 logical pixels on each side, so its measured outer aspect is slightly different from 16:9. World sprites retain uniform scale; this does not stretch a screenshot or reveal more vertical world.

During STATE_PLAY the existing camera uses the wider logical extent; the original resize/cache invalidation allocates matching raster surfaces. Once play begins, Login, None and Transfer phases retain the wide frame even when a second, pending Game is still logging in. The presentation hook runs before drawing, avoiding a narrow intermediate frame. Both shared UI roots stay at their original world origin. Independent screen roots anchor the health/hotbar left and chat/top HUD right. The quest panel extends through the new center space, widening text fields without scaling their fonts. Utility buttons distribute across that space individually; party controls retain their original left anchor. The Map tooltip and initial Map cover keep their native proportions. The independently attached social window centers separately. Building inspection replaces the complete combat HUD: its upgrade strip anchors at the far left, with neutral native backing continuing to the map panel. Health, resources, XP, powers and companion ornament restore when inspection closes; the actual panel width determines any additional map clearance.

Registered full-view modal mattes extend to cover the widened scene without scaling their panels, text, controls or opacity. The matching tolerance includes the original building matte's small inset; two logical pixels of overscan prevent an exposed edge. Weak-key saved geometry restores when widescreen turns off; game-owned repositioning replaces the offset baseline instead of accumulating it. Flash's 1/20-pixel coordinate quantization is respected.

Home interiors retain the original room, camera, player and door geometry; the world canvas is never clipped to conceal uncovered scenery or players. Reviewed passive wall/floor artwork fills only the area outside the native room background, underneath terrain and actors. It is rasterized at native resolution and tiled, rather than stretching a cached bitmap. Only the active room/scale raster is retained; allocations are bounded and disposed when replaced. No playable map data or generated terrain is added. The backmost passive sky/cloud plane is separately rasterized from its original vector to cover the extra width; buildings, terrain and entities keep their original proportions. The persistent final canvas is cleared only when the game is about to redraw, preventing old roofs/scenery from remaining in newly uncovered edges; frozen Map/Forge frames retain their original behavior.

The original SuperAnimData.method_200 added cropped raster pixel coordinates directly to an inverse transform in logical game units. At a render scale other than one this displaced cached artwork, including NPC names. A whole-hash-guarded 12-byte call replacement sends the crop origin through the complete inverse matrix (including both axes) before translation. Branch and exception addresses remain unchanged. A baseline runs the original body; the corrected real a_PlayerName cache matches direct vector bounds at 0.5, 1, 1.4, 2 and 3 scales. This is a demonstrated cache-origin correction, not proof that every private live map's seam comes from that same bug.

The loaded game's original a_EdgeHud vector artwork replaces the plain frame from 0.11.0. Three clipped decorative instances preserve both corners/side sections and extend only the horizontal middle, retaining cracks and shading. Its lower decoration stops at the shared divider; the gray outer highlight no longer extends over the HUD. Another three-slice original a_Hud decoration extends the neutral bottom backing while preserving health/power and chat proportions. Normal play keeps the open curved health surround; rectangular left backing exists only during inspection. The enlarged center's duplicate upper rim is clipped without moving its controls or extending fill into the divider. Building inspection replaces only the reviewed passive outer backing with the shared native HUD plate, preserving its bevel without a duplicate corner or missing border. Headings, prices and Upgrade controls stay intact. Native visibility restores when widescreen is disabled. A muted sage fill under only the enlarged center prevents the old utility-slot cutouts exposing gray gaps between the redistributed buttons. All nested decorative timelines are stopped, matching the original game's static-cache behavior; stopping only their root left uncontrolled child timelines running. No game frames are captured for production rendering. Both decorations accept no input and retain original vector rendering.

The 0.11.0 experiment missed two assumptions in the original terrain renderer. class_23.method_942 allocates ceil(576 * rasterScale), while method_1753 compared that integer against the unrounded product. Widescreen's fractional scale caused the entire terrain bitmap pool to be destroyed and allocated on every frame. The exact-hash guarded patch now rounds the comparison the same way as allocation; its 14-byte replacement preserves all branch/exception offsets and unrelated method bodies. The original four half-tile columns could also fail to cover the wider view when a lower staggered tile row was partly visible. Widescreen now requests six columns, preserving the even-count row algorithm, original tile sizes and pool budget. Switching back restores four.

The October 10 client's transition allocator had replaced the original scaled camera dimensions with a fixed 2048 x 1152 bitmap. At ordinary 1080p/1440p this mismatches the launcher's raster surface and can spill into, or leave seams beside, the gray margins. The reviewed correction restores the original allocator byte-for-byte. The isolated regression calls the actual Game.method_1947, reproduces the bad allocation in a baseline fixture, then checks matching dimensions after the correction. The original and corrected method bodies are full-hash checked, and the 38-byte substitution preserves branch/exception offsets.

Validation uses a guarded inactive Windows desktop. A baseline with the original method reproduces a cache rebuild on every rendered frame. Another baseline reproduces undrawn right-edge pixels with four-column coverage and a partly visible second row. The corrected actual class_23 cache and Game.method_1946 renderer reuse cache identity. Solid-region checks sample every raster column at five vertical positions, across all three detail levels. Extended travel covers 23,000 logical horizontal pixels and six vertical positions, including fractional coordinates and a nonzero level origin. The smaller disposable pool forces repeated tile reuse; one fixture condition deliberately ties timestamps without changing any production clock. With the original start+2 protection range that condition reproduces visible terrain corruption. A whole-hash-checked single operand in class_23.method_1389 now protects through start+6, covering all six staggered half-columns plus boundary prefetch. The original pool budget, allocations, cache keys, timestamps and clocks remain unchanged; original-width rendering retains the extra protected ring. The baseline reports incorrect sampled pixels while the corrected renderer reports zero, including the tied-clock condition. This is not a measured combat speedup or proof that every reported live terrain artifact is fixed.

Original HUD, chat, party and both idol/trove modal assets render in test-only wiring that skips network/player initialization. Tests check separate anchors, label/party origins, both matte extents, restoration, input and long camera travel. They also assert menu visibility, actual camera coordinates and both rendered outer corners' dimming, while preserving panel/pedestal widths. A stale legacy app.asar initially ignored newly added test commands; the runner now rebuilds it and verifies every legacy source file against the runtime archive before launching. Those older apparent passes were not accepted as evidence. Fresh public Home/UI assets were fetched on October 10, separately from the historical checkout; none are committed or bundled.

Additional checks cover real Flash layout/transition allocation at 1080p/1440p, repeated offset stability, left/center/right input conversion, frame click-through, zoom, fullscreen, toggling off and returning to menus. The real live title renders with widescreen enabled while retaining original artwork. Software-rendered captures are of test WebContents only; no input desktop capture, desktop switching, clipboard access or ClipCursor call is made. Automated fixtures are unauthenticated. Separately authorized private live QA uses one selected-origin save in a disposable profile; account values and captures are never published. Private live Paladin views exercise initialized HUD/menu rendering, movement, Home exterior, Great Hall, Library, World Map and Tome of Power inspection. These do not establish every encounter, private dungeon or long-session performance. Hatchery inspection uses original-asset fixtures.

This is not validated against every live authenticated HUD, dungeon transition or encounter. Some maps may reveal blank/unfinished scenery, camera clamps may be designed for the original width, and complex fixed-width overlays may need additional positioning. Use the original layout if an overlay is misplaced. The preservation server does not advertise support for this modification; no claim of official compatibility, FPS gain or altered gameplay rules is made.

## Vertical artwork investigation

Sixteen public Home-room assets have overall bounds between roughly 806 and 1,369 logical pixels high; Home itself is about 1,083 high. Those bounds also include props, collision geometry and spawn/marker objects, so they do not establish continuous visible scenery above/below the current camera. Village starts at logical y=140, and other rooms have negative origins. Dungeon rooms and private live camera constraints have not been exhaustively validated. A universal taller viewport would require camera clamps, terrain row coverage, transition allocation, input coordinates and HUD placement to be reviewed together. Consequently this patch keeps the original 668-pixel world height and bottom HUD; it does not stretch art or expose unverified vertical space.


The quest backing uses a passive nine-slice original vector plate instead of resizing the original cache container. Repeated calls to the actual cache rebuilder retain its original width within raster rounding. The quest text, progress line and six utility/direction slots share one plate, stopping above the HUD's lower bevel. The plate is approximately 20 logical pixels narrower with matching 10-pixel outer gaps. Smaller utilities leave padding below the progress line. Measured icon faces, excluding hidden tooltips, give equal visible gaps between the narrower direction circle and the square utility buttons. A muted green dash occupies the direction slot when the native arrow is unavailable. Locked talent stones retain a disabled slot. Social count text stays within the original icon, five logical pixels lower. Pet and mount icons match ability size; a generated gold companion ornament uses the supplied 2008 HUD as its style reference, with a slim three-pixel lower bevel. The original ability groove and lower lip end at their native edge; only the narrow XP channel extends under companions. The gold companion ornament is a clearly separate unit. Resource bars retain native lengths during play.

The companion ornament is separate from the native ability/XP groove.
